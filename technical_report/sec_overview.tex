\section{Solution Overview \& AI-Assisted Development}
\label{sec:overview}

\paragraph{Development methodology.}
The entire kernel --- routing logic, Triton kernels, dispatch optimization, precision analysis, and NCU-guided tuning --- was developed in an \textbf{agent-assisted workflow}.
An AI agent iteratively generated candidate kernels, ran them against the FlashInfer-Bench evaluation framework~\cite{flashinfer} on Modal B200 instances, analyzed benchmark outputs and NCU profiles, identified bottlenecks, and proposed the next optimization.
Human oversight focused on directing search, validating precision rules, and resolving numerical failures.
The codebase, notes, ideas, and all benchmark logs are available at \url{https://github.com/VanshNawander/accelerated-hpc}.

\paragraph{Precision discipline.}
A key invariant discovered during development:
\[
  \underbrace{\mathrm{FP8}}_{\text{load}}
  \xrightarrow[\text{lossless}]{\text{BF16 cast}}
  \underbrace{\mathrm{BF16}}_{\cdot}
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{tl.dot}}
  \underbrace{\mathrm{FP32}}_{\text{raw sum}}
  \xrightarrow{\times S_a S_w}
  \underbrace{\mathrm{FP32}}_{\text{acc}}
  \xrightarrow{\text{cast}}
  \underbrace{\mathrm{BF16}}_{\text{out}}
\]
FP8 E4M3 has only 4 significant mantissa bits, fitting losslessly into BF16 (7 bits).
Consequently, \emph{any FP32-computed value} (SwiGLU activations, accumulated GEMM outputs) must remain FP32 until the final output cast --- violating this rule caused 18/19 correctness failures in one iteration (Section~\ref{sec:failures}).
